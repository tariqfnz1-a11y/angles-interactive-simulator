What is an angle?

An angle is made when two lines (called arms) meet at a point (the vertex). We measure angles in degrees (°).

  • Vertex: the meeting point
  • Arms: the two lines forming the angle
  • Unit: degrees (°)

Angle types (with colours)

A
Acute angle — less than 90° (small & sharp)
Examples: 30°, 45°
R
Right angle — exactly 90° (perfect corner). Symbol: ⟂
O
Obtuse angle — greater than 90° but less than 180° (wide). Example: 120°
S
Straight angle — exactly 180° (a straight line)
R
Reflex angle — greater than 180° but less than 360° (bends the other way)
F
Full / Complete angle — exactly 360° (all the way round)

Tips to recognise angles

  • If the angle is smaller than the corner of a book, it is probably acute.
  • If it looks like the corner of a square, it is a right angle.
  • If it opens more than a right angle but is not a straight line, it is obtuse.

Practice questions

  1. Is 45° acute, right, obtuse, straight, or reflex?
  2. Name the angle type for 90°.
  3. What type is 200°?
